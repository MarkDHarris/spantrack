Notes from the Ridge

On 14 March 1997, at 4:32 a.m., Dr. Okoro logged the comet as it neared perihelion. It was moving at roughly 47.5 km/s and had brightened to magnitude 1.2 overnight.

The coma spanned about 3,000 km; the tail, nearly 1.5 million. We paid $1,240 for the week’s telescope time on Mt. Wilson, and it was worth every cent — a sight like this returns only once every 2,533 years.

By aphelion it will be a faint smudge again, No. 7 in a catalogue no one reads… until the next return.
